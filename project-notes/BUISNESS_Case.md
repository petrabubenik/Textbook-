## The Idea 
The specific customer is low income university students. Every university has classes and textbooks. Over time they get very expensive, and most students end up never looking at their old textbooks again. This website gives lower income students the same opportunity to have resources, like other students. These options all have pretty similar layouts but a lot of students trade cheap prices for trust. Facebook, Kijiji, and friends are the cheapest options but also unorganized. Online retailers have a lot of selection but aren’t specific to a university or campus. None of these options let students trust who they are getting the books from at a good price in one place. Campus Books allow students to complete a sale in one place and find the best prices. Being able to pick up a textbook down the street at a fellow students house trusts who you get your textbooks from, allowing students not to have to give up trust for a good price. Sellers pay a 12% fee on each completed sale or rental, this is taken automatically from the payment. Students pay the listed price on the website with no added fees. Students only buy a few books for every semester so a monthly subscription doesn’t seem fair. A fee per sale means nobody gets paid unless someone buys or rents a book from the website. If your book doesn’t sell, you don’t pay anything. Sellers get more than the bookstores buyback offers, and buyers pay less for a new book.
## Where IT Creates Value 
If a student wants to buy a textbook they spend days looking through different websites like Kijij, Facebook, and online retailers comparing prices and signing up for multiple apps. Campus Books puts buyers and sellers all in one place and can search by course code. A student can type “BSAD 281” and find every available textbook and any given price. This widens reach for sellers, who get seen by students at their university and it lowers costs for buyers who can compare prices in one place. Without software each sale means a long chain of messages like asking if the books are still available, when to pick up or drop off, and trusting a stranger. Campus Books handles the listings, payments, and the 12% automatic fee. This allows students to not have to message back and forth constantly, you can decide a place and pick up. Over time the platforms campus relationships would grow stronger, and network effects would help enforce this since sellers attract buyers and buyers attract sellers. 
## Network Effects
Campus Books is a two - sided market, the sellers are one side (students posting textbooks) and the other side is the buyers and renters. It is local, it’s hard meeting or trading books with students of different universities so every campus is a small network. My network effect I would say is weak to moderate. It is very local so every campus is starting from zero, it's very seasonal so the start of semesters would be very busy then it would be very slow for the rest of the months, and multiple other platforms. Moderate because it's a two -sided market, and the trust will add value. With 5 students on the site looking for a specific book will probably now find anything and go back to the bookstore. With 500 students on the site, buyers can see more options and compare more prices to find the one that works best for them. This goes for sellers, with no students the listings just sit on the site but more buyers means the listings will sell quickly. Each buyer is useful to the sellers, and each seller is useful to the buyers. I would start on a small campus, and choose a high - enrolled degree like business. I would start by reaching out to sellers, students finishing up their terms that no longer need their books. Reaching out at the end of term through posters, or group chats. Once there are enough listings students arriving next semester get real options to choose from. The browse page shows where students can see all the textbooks and search for their specific courses.
## Can It Be Copied
Yes, Campus Books can be easily rebuilt by AI in a weekend. If Campus Books has an advantage it would come from resources a competitor can’t copy, so I tested it against VRIN valuable, rare, inimitable, non substitutable. 
One resource is the local listings, so how many books are available on each campus. It is valuable because a buyer only returns if the book they want is on the website. At the start no one has it before launch making it rare. It’s only a little hard to imitate, other rivals can recruit sellers the same way I would. It’s not non-substitutable because Facebook and Kijiji already hold listings, giving Campus Books a bit of an advantage. Another resource is the trust and reputation data, like seller ratings and verified students. Buying from a stranger is very difficult, so buying from a trusted student makes this valuable. Rating only exists on our site, and new competitors would start with none making this rare. It's hard to imitate because ratings can’t be copied and takes a while to build. Friends recommendations still replace it for some students making it mostly non-substitutable. The last resource is partnerships with students and campus. This is valuable because it gives Campus books credibility, it's rare and non-substitutable if it is exclusive. Relationships take time to build, but rivals can just offer better prices making it only partly hard to imitate. 
There are a few weak spots including, Facebook and Kijiji are free substitutes that students already use, and switching costs are low because sellers have listings on multiple different platforms. Demand is seasonal and books are cheap, so each sale earns a little. Campus Books will not have a secure advantage in the market. A rival would need to copy the site, recruit sellers, and fill the courses on each campus. A rival could move faster by paying students to list books or lowering fees, but that costs money for very little profit per sale, so big companies are unlikely to bother. Over time, trust and campus relationships would get stronger, but the code wouldn’t. This gives Campus Books a more lasting advantage, the early lead would only be temporary but I would focus on being the most trusted option on campuses.
## Cloud and Operations
A real Campus Books needs a database, payments, and email notifications. Payment processing, notifications, and login/user accounts would be SaaS, so a software we rent and use as is. The prototype is on GitHub, which is closer to PaaS, and a real version would need a database to store listings, users, and ratings. We would build an app, and the provider would handle the servers. We would not use IaaS at first, because it takes more work to manage and only makes sense once we outgrow the platform. 
The biggest risk is a failure at the start of a semester, when most books are bought and sold. If students can’t list or pay, then many would go back to Facebook or Kijiji. To stop this from happening I would keep the site simple and test it before each term, and have a backup plan like a page that still shows listings if payment stops working. If a provider raises its prices our 12% will still remain the same, so I would pick providers that charge based on use and switch if I had to. 
## Risks and Ethics
The Campus Books site holds students names, university emails, messages, listing history, ratings, and payment details. This increases risks for scams or fake listings, like any purchasing app seller could post a book they don't own, or a buyer could purchase a book and never receive it. To reduce this risk, we would require a university email to sign up, hold payments until the buyer confirms they have the book. Another risk is unsafe meetups, since students would be meeting up with strangers to exchange books. One thing about Campus Books is every stranger is still a student at your same school but I’d recommend meeting in public places like libraries, or somewhere on campus so you don’t share your address. Last risk is your data getting leaked, students emails, contact info, and payment info scammers can get a hold of and use them. I would collect data only that is needed, use payment providers so payment info isn’t stored on site, and give students the options to delete accounts and messages.
